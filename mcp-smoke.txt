[CHUNK 1 | pages 1–2]
Goal: estimate the Hurst exponent H of self-affine time series (fractional
Brownian motion, fBm) using Quantile Graphs (QGs).

Key claims (Abstract / Highlights):
• A QG is built by mapping the Q quantiles of a time series into Q nodes of a
  directed, weighted graph.
• H is computed as the power-law scaling exponent of the mean jump length
  Δ(k) performed by a random walker on the QG across time differences k.
• The method is numerically simple with a single free parameter Q
  (number of quantiles/nodes).
• With a cumulative-sum pre-processing step the method extends to fractional
  Gaussian noises (fGn).
• Comparison of Ĥ_QG against exact H values shows excellent agreement.

[CHUNK 2 | pages 2–3]
Section 2 — Methods (QG construction):
• Coarse-grain the time-series value range into Q quantiles q_1,…,q_Q.
• Map M: time series X → network g = {N, A}, where each quantile q_i → node n_i.
• Two nodes n_i and n_j are connected by a weighted arc whenever x(t) ∈ q_i
  and x(t+k) ∈ q_j, for t = 1,…,T and k = 1,…,k_max < T.
• Arc weight w_ij^k = count of transitions from q_i to q_j at lag k,
  normalised by total transitions → Markov transition matrix W_k
  (row-stochastic: Σ_j w_ij^k = 1).
• Network is weighted, directed, connected.

Random-walk & H estimation (equation 1, page 3):
  Δ(k) = (1/L) Σ_{l=1}^{L} δ_{l,k}(i,j),   where δ_{l,k}(i,j) = |i − j|
  For fBm:  Δ(k) ~ k^H  (power-law scaling).
  H is extracted from the slope of a log–log plot of Δ(k) vs k via
  linear regression on log-transformed coordinates.

[CHUNK 3 | pages 2–3]  (Introduction context)
• fBm: non-stationary, self-affine, power-spectrum P(ω) ~ ω^{-β},
  with 1 < β ≤ 3 for motions, −1 ≤ β ≤ 1 for noises.
• Relationship: β = 2H+1 for motions, β = 2H−1 for noises.
• Classical Brownian motion: H = 1/2.
• Positive long-range correlations: H > 1/2; anti-correlations: H < 1/2.
• For noises, cumulative sum converts fGn → fBm before applying QG.

[CHUNK 4 | pages 3–4]
Section 3 — Results:
• Synthetic data generated with wavelet-based synthesis [Ref 23].
• Default parameters: Q = 50, T = 10,000, averaged over 100 realisations.
• Power-law scaling of Δ(k) spans more than two decades (Fig. 3).
• Linear regression [Refs 24,25] on log-transformed Δ(k) vs k gives Ĥ_QG;
  error bars = 95% confidence intervals.
• Strong linear correlation between Ĥ_QG and exact H confirmed (Fig. 4).
• Q sensitivity: |Ĥ_QG − H| < 0.02 for all β at Q = 50 and Q = 100 (Fig. 5).
• Computational cost grows linearly with Q for fixed T.

Section 4 — Conclusions:
• QG method successfully estimates H for fBm and (with modification) fGn.
• Single free parameter Q; robust estimation; error driven by power-law
  fitting framework, not by Q choice.
